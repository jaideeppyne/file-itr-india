# Foreign assets — Schedule FA

Common for anyone at a startup with an overseas parent: founder shares or ESOPs in
a US company, a foreign brokerage account, an overseas bank account.

**The obligation attaches to *holding* the asset, not to earning from it.** A
shareholding that paid no dividend and was never sold still gets disclosed in full.
Omission — not the tax — is what carries Black Money Act exposure.

Schedule FA applies to a **resident and ordinarily resident**. An RNOR or
non-resident does not file it.

## The calendar-year trap

**Schedule FA reports the calendar year, not the financial year.** For AY 2026-27
that is **1 January to 31 December 2025**, while every other schedule in the same
return covers 1 April 2025 to 31 March 2026.

Every table header states its period. Read it before entering a date, peak value
or income figure — a value taken from the FY is simply the wrong number.

## Which table

| Table | Holds |
|---|---|
| **A1** | Foreign depository accounts |
| **A2** | Foreign custodial accounts |
| **A3** | **Foreign equity and debt interest** — shares in a foreign company |
| **A4** | Foreign cash-value insurance or annuity contracts |
| **B** | Financial interest in any entity |
| **C** | Immovable property |
| **D** | Any other capital asset |
| **E** | Accounts with signing authority, not already in A–D |
| **F** | Trusts where the taxpayer is trustee, beneficiary or settlor |
| **G** | Any other income from a source outside India |

Shares in a foreign company are **equity**, so **A3** is the precise table. Table B
is the broader "financial interest" catch-all — the right home for an LLC
membership, a partnership interest, or a beneficial interest that is not
straightforward equity.

Where prior years reported a shareholding in **B**, reporting in **A3 as well as**
B keeps continuity while putting the holding in its proper table. Income is nil in
both, so nothing double-counts.

Table A3 wants initial value, **peak value during the period**, and closing value.
Table B wants a single total investment at cost. Table E covers signing authority
over an account whose money is not the taxpayer's — a company account they operate
as a director still gets disclosed.

## Valuing an unlisted foreign holding

Where there is no market quotation and no priced round, **acquisition cost** is the
defensible basis for initial, peak and closing value, applied consistently.

Founder shares issued for **no cash consideration** genuinely carry a cost of ₹0,
and ₹0 is then the accurate entry rather than an understatement. Confirm which it
is — "nominal value" usually means a small real payment (a Delaware founder buying
restricted stock at par to start the holding period and support an 83(b) election),
and that amount converted at the acquisition-date rate is the correct figure.

Where a priced round has happened and the taxpayer prefers that basis, apply it
consistently across all three values and keep the valuation documentable.

## Unlisted equity shares — a separate schedule

Holding shares in **any** unlisted company, Indian or foreign, triggers two things
in ITR-2/ITR-3 beyond Schedule FA:

- It **bars ITR-1 and ITR-4**.
- The **Unlisted Equity Shares** schedule in Part A-General must be filled: company
  name, type (there is a **Foreign** option), PAN (**not mandatory** — leave blank
  for a foreign company), opening and closing share count, and cost.

Ask for **shares actually issued and held**, not the fully-diluted number.
"Fully diluted" ordinarily includes unexercised options, which are not held shares.

## Income, and when there is none

- **No dividend and no sale** — the schedule is pure disclosure, and total income is
  unchanged. Table B's "Schedule where offered" takes *No Income during the year*.
- **Dividend** — taxable in India at slab rates as foreign income. Where foreign tax
  was withheld, Schedule FSI and Schedule TR carry the credit, and Form 67 must be
  filed to claim it.
- **Sale** — capital gains taxable in India. Unlisted foreign shares are long-term
  after 24 months.

## How acquisition affects the rest of the return

- **Founder or early shares at nominal value** — no perquisite, cost basis is what
  was paid.
- **ESOP or RSU that vested during the year** — vesting is a taxable perquisite
  under s.17(2), which should appear in Form 16. A vesting event with ₹0 perquisite
  on Form 16 is a gap to resolve before filing.
- **Bought for cash** — no perquisite. Where the purchase was funded from India,
  expect a matching LRS remittance and a s.206CQ TCS entry in 26AS; its absence
  means the holding was funded earlier or from outside India.

## Flagging it in Part B-TTI

Part B-TTI carries a direct question: whether the taxpayer held any asset outside
India, had signing authority over a foreign account, or had income from a foreign
source. Answer **Yes** wherever Schedule FA is filled — the two must agree, and a
mismatch is a defect.
